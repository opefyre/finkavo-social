# Dec 2026 experiment: assignment and scripts (v2, audited 23 Nov 2026)

Topics randomly assigned to the 24 runs (seed 20261123). Style for all: the 2D cartoon cast (Otto the expat, Avó the
grandma, Dona the clerk, Buck the neighbour), narrator + character lines, our own music, 15:00 Lisbon.

## Story rules (every script)
One protagonist with one want. Three steps that each make it worse (escalation, a new picture every 1.2–3 s). A payoff in
the last 25% that answers the hook or flips the situation. A short button (a reaction, ending in motion).
- **Question hook:** the payoff *is* the answer. **Warning hook:** the story shows what happens when you ignore it.
- **Fact runs:** the verified fact is the turn of the story (the character uses it to win, or learns it the hard way),
  not a card stuck on the end. Wording from docs/experiment-research/summary.md.
- **Comedy runs:** a punchline payoff and **no rule, number or legal claim** (keeps the content factor clean).
- **Length:** 14 s ≈ 32–38 spoken words; 20 s ≈ 48–55 words. One subtitled Portuguese line per reel.
- Nothing on screen implies an unverified rule.

## Audit of v1 (what was wrong, fixed below)
1. **Fact runs bolted the fact on as a card before the button** (all 12). Now the fact is the turning point.
2. **Gag lists, not stories:** car inspection (three tests in a row), condo meeting, vet bill, phone contract, CTT. Rewritten
   with a want, rising stakes and one payoff.
3. **Question hooks left unanswered:** CTT ("how without knocking?"), car inspection ("why pray?"), card café ("why stare?").
   Each payoff now answers its question.
4. **Implied unverified rules in comedy runs:** the card café ("€5 minimum") and proof of address (neighbours as witnesses,
   unverified). Removed.
5. **Incoherent hook vs fact:** baptism ("don't put a cheque") had nothing to do with stamp duty. Rewritten.
6. **Thin setups:** gas bottle and Christmas bonus had no obstacle. Rewritten with a want and a twist.

## Scripts
Format: **Hook** (frame 0 + narrator line) · **Want** · **Escalation** (3 steps) · **Payoff** · **Button**.
N = narrator, O = Otto, A = Avó, D = Dona, B = Buck. [PT] = subtitled Portuguese.

### 08 Dec · Landlord wants cash · fact / warning / 14 s
- **Hook:** O holds out a thick envelope at a door. N: "Never pay your rent in an envelope."
- **Want:** O wants to keep his landlord happy.
- **Escalation:** the landlord grabs the envelope, counts it twice; O: "Receipt?"; the landlord: "Recibo? [PT] (A receipt?)" and
  laughs, closing the door.
- **Payoff (fact):** O, at tax time, sees an empty rent box. N: "Renters get 15% of their rent back on IRS, up to €900 for 2026."
- **Button:** O knocks again, louder: "RECIBO!"

### 09 Dec · Pharmacy queue · comedy / warning / 14 s
- **Hook:** O, sweating, holds one box of paracetamol in a long pharmacy queue. N: "Never go to a Portuguese pharmacy in a hurry."
- **Want:** O just needs to pay and run.
- **Escalation:** the lady at the counter talks about her knee; then her son; then pulls out photos of her grandchildren.
- **Payoff:** it's finally O's turn; the pharmacist leans in, smiling: "So… and how is *your* family?"
- **Button:** O, defeated, pulls out his phone photos.

### 10 Dec · "Only a voucher" · fact / warning / 20 s
- **Hook:** a new toaster launches toast at O's face. N: "Don't accept 'only a voucher' for something broken."
- **Want:** O wants his money back.
- **Escalation:** at the shop, the clerk: "Só vale. [PT] (Only a voucher.)"; O insists; the clerk points to a sign "No refunds".
- **Turn (fact):** A arrives with the receipt. N: "Broken goods have a 3-year guarantee. And bought online? 14 days to change your mind."
- **Payoff:** the clerk swaps the toaster for a working one.
- **Button:** the new toaster pops, toast lands perfectly on A's plate.

### 11 Dec · School books · fact / question / 20 s
- **Hook:** B kisses a crumpled receipt. N: "Why do Portuguese parents keep every school receipt?"
- **Want:** O, new dad, just wants the €312 September book list behind him.
- **Escalation:** he throws the receipt away; B gasps; B digs it out of the bin; O is confused.
- **Payoff (answer = fact):** B, proudly: N: "School costs give you 30% back on your IRS, up to €800 a household."
- **Button:** O dives into his own bin.

### 12 Dec · Black Friday "–70%" · comedy / warning / 20 s
- **Hook:** a TV with a giant "–50%" sticker, O running at it. N: "Don't fall for the Friday sticker."
- **Want:** O wants the deal of the year.
- **Escalation:** he grabs the last TV; fights a man for it; wins; carries it home in the rain.
- **Payoff:** at home A shows him last week's flyer: the same TV, same price. "Ai, filho… [PT] (Oh, son…)"
- **Button:** O, soaked, hugging the TV.

### 13 Dec · Bakery weighs the bread · comedy / question / 14 s
- **Hook:** a baker weighs one bread roll on a scale like gold. N: "How much is one bread roll in Portugal?"
- **Want:** O wants one roll, fast.
- **Escalation:** the baker adds a crumb, removes a crumb; the whole queue holds its breath.
- **Payoff (answer):** the display: €0.17. O pays with a €20 note. The whole shop turns.
- **Button:** baker: "Não tem trocado? [PT] (No change?)"

### 14 Dec · Electricity estimate · fact / question / 14 s
- **Hook:** O opens a bill, it's huge, the word ESTIMATED glows. N: "Why did Otto's bill triple while he was away?"
- **Want:** O wants to pay only for what he used.
- **Escalation:** he was on holiday all month; the meter hasn't moved; he calls; hold music.
- **Payoff (answer = fact):** N: "It was an estimate. Send your own meter reading; corrections can't go back over 6 months."
- **Button:** O photographs the meter like a paparazzo.

### 15 Dec · CTT "we tried to deliver" · comedy / question / 20 s
- **Hook:** O glued to his peephole, a calendar marked "PARCEL TODAY". N: "How does a parcel get delivered without anyone knocking?"
- **Want:** O will not miss his parcel.
- **Escalation:** he waits all morning; skips lunch; sets three alarms; the doorbell never rings.
- **Payoff (answer):** he sneezes, closes his eyes for one second: a "we tried to deliver" note slides under the door. The answer: it never was.
- **Button:** O stares at the note; the van drives past the window.

### 16 Dec · Condo meeting · comedy / warning / 14 s
- **Hook:** O enters a room of neighbours with folders and a projector. N: "Never go to your first condomínio meeting."
- **Want:** O wants to propose fixing the hallway light.
- **Escalation:** the debate over the bulb's colour lasts an hour; then a vote; then a recount.
- **Payoff:** everyone else has quietly left; O is the only one there, and is elected administrator.
- **Button:** A, from the door: "Parabéns! [PT] (Congratulations!)"

### 17 Dec · Vet bill · comedy / warning / 20 s
- **Hook:** the cat sits calmly on the vet table; O smiling. N: "Don't take your cat to the vet just to say hello."
- **Want:** O wants a quick check-up.
- **Escalation:** the vet adds a vaccine; then a blood test; then "a consultation about the consultation"; the bill unrolls to the floor.
- **Payoff:** O pays; the cat was fine all along and walks out first, tail up.
- **Button:** A to the cat: "Gato de luxo. [PT] (Luxury cat.)"

### 18 Dec · Grandma's raspadinha · fact / question / 14 s
- **Hook:** A scratching a raspadinha with a lucky coin. N: "What happens if grandma wins big?"
- **Want:** A wants the jackpot.
- **Escalation:** first number matches; second; third: €10,000! She dances with the café owner.
- **Payoff (answer = fact):** N: "Santa Casa prizes pay 20% tax on the part above €5,000. She still takes home €9,000."
- **Button:** A: "Ainda dá para muita bica. [PT] (Still buys a lot of coffee.)"

### 19 Dec · Baptism envelope · fact / warning / 20 s
- **Hook:** O at a baptism, staring at a mountain of envelopes. N: "Don't panic about the baptism envelope."
- **Want:** O wants to give the right gift as the family friend.
- **Escalation:** the grandparents hand over a fat envelope; O worries about tax on money gifts; he asks A, sweating.
- **Turn (fact):** A laughs. N: "Money from parents or grandparents? 0% stamp duty. Other people's big gifts can pay 10%."
- **Payoff:** O, relieved, hands over his envelope: a drawing of the baby.
- **Button:** the baby grabs it and smiles.

### 20 Dec · Christmas bonus · fact / warning / 14 s
- **Hook:** O imagining palm trees while waving a payslip envelope. N: "Don't book the trip before you open the payslip."
- **Want:** O wants to spend his Christmas bonus on the beach.
- **Escalation:** he packs; he buys sunglasses; he opens the payslip: smaller than he thought.
- **Payoff (fact):** N: "The Christmas bonus is taxed on its own, never added to your salary."
- **Button:** O on a Lisbon bench in sunglasses with a bica.

### 21 Dec · No receipt at the café · comedy / question / 14 s
- **Hook:** O at a café counter, one espresso. N: "What happens if you ask for a receipt for one coffee?"
- **Want:** O just wants his receipt.
- **Escalation:** "Fatura, por favor." The café goes silent; the machine stops; the barman prints slowly.
- **Payoff (answer):** the receipt is a metre long.
- **Button:** A at the next table: "Com contribuinte? [PT] (With your tax number?)"

### 22 Dec · Car inspection panic · comedy / question / 20 s
- **Hook:** O kissing his rusty car's bonnet outside the inspection centre. N: "Why do Portuguese drivers pray before the inspection?"
- **Want:** O's old car must pass.
- **Escalation:** a hubcap rolls away; the horn plays a sad fado note; the inspector walks around it twice, writing.
- **Payoff (answer):** "Aprovado. [PT] (Passed.)" O cheers; the car door falls off behind him. That's why they pray.
- **Button:** O drives off holding the door.

### 23 Dec · Bank fees · fact / question / 20 s
- **Hook:** O's bank statement with "fee", "fee", "fee for the fee". N: "How much should a basic bank account cost a year?"
- **Want:** O wants to stop losing money every month.
- **Escalation:** he counts the fees; A shows her bank book, almost nothing taken; he's furious.
- **Payoff (answer = fact):** N: "A basic bank account costs at most €5.37 a year."
- **Button:** O at the counter: "Serviços mínimos, por favor. [PT] (The basic one, please.)"

### 24 Dec · Gas bottle guy · fact / warning / 14 s
- **Hook:** O shivering in a cold flat, an empty gas bottle. N: "Don't pay full price for your gas bottle this winter."
- **Want:** O wants heat for Christmas Eve.
- **Escalation:** he hears the gas van's horn; he runs down; he's last in line.
- **Payoff (fact):** A, ahead of him, whispers. N: "Bottled gas support: €25 back per bottle, until 31 December."
- **Button:** O and A carry bottles home, warm and smug.

### 25 Dec · Splitting the tasca bill · comedy / warning / 14 s
- **Hook:** nine relatives at a Christmas lunch, the bill lands. N: "Never try to pay at a Portuguese family lunch."
- **Want:** O wants to pay, to be polite.
- **Escalation:** he reaches for the bill; four hands grab it; a tug of war across the table.
- **Payoff:** A returns from the kitchen: it's already paid. She winks.
- **Button:** O still holding a torn piece of the bill.

### 26 Dec · New puppy · fact / warning / 20 s
- **Hook:** a puppy in a Christmas box, O delighted. N: "Got a puppy for Christmas? Don't skip this."
- **Want:** O wants the perfect first week with the dog.
- **Escalation:** the puppy wrecks the sofa; eats a shoe; at the park a neighbour asks: "Tem chip? [PT] (Does it have a chip?)"
- **Turn (fact):** N: "Dogs and cats need a microchip and registration within 120 days of birth. Fines go up to €3,740."
- **Payoff:** at the vet, the puppy gets its chip, proud.
- **Button:** the puppy chews the registration paper.

### 27 Dec · Paying a café by card · comedy / question / 14 s
- **Hook:** O holding out a card for an €0.80 espresso. N: "Why does the barman sigh when you pay by card?"
- **Want:** O has no cash, just a card.
- **Escalation:** the barman sighs; searches a drawer; untangles a card machine from 1999.
- **Payoff (answer):** the machine prints its own receipt longer than the menu, and the battery dies. That's why.
- **Button:** O pays in coins found in A's pocket.

### 28 Dec · Phone contract · comedy / warning / 20 s
- **Hook:** O signing a phone contract with a big smile, a free phone in hand. N: "Don't sign the 'free phone' deal in a hurry."
- **Want:** O wants a cheap new phone.
- **Escalation:** months later he wants to leave; the shop assistant smiles; he calls the helpline; hold music; the same song, for an hour.
- **Payoff:** he gives up and keeps the contract; the "free" phone's screen cracks.
- **Button:** A, with a phone from 1998: "O meu nunca falha. [PT] (Mine never fails.)"

### 29 Dec · Grandpa pays cash · fact / question / 14 s
- **Hook:** grandpa opening a suitcase full of notes at a car dealer. N: "Can grandpa buy a car with a suitcase of cash?"
- **Want:** grandpa wants his dream car, paid his way.
- **Escalation:** the salesman sweats; the manager runs over; they both shake their heads.
- **Payoff (answer = fact):** N: "No. Paying €3,000 or more in cash is illegal in Portugal."
- **Button:** grandpa closes the suitcase: "Então um triciclo. [PT] (A tricycle, then.)"

### 30 Dec · Complaints book · fact / question / 20 s
- **Hook:** O at a restaurant with the wrong dish, for the third time. N: "What's the most powerful book in Portugal?"
- **Want:** O just wants the dish he ordered.
- **Escalation:** the waiter ignores him; the manager shrugs; O calmly says: "Livro de reclamações. [PT] (Complaints book.)"
- **Payoff (answer = fact):** the room freezes; the manager runs over with his dish and a free dessert. N: "Every shop must have one, paper and online. Fines start at €650."
- **Button:** O eats the dessert, smiling.

### 31 Dec · Proof of address · comedy / question / 20 s
- **Hook:** O at a counter holding a tower of papers. N: "How do you prove where you live in Portugal?"
- **Want:** O needs one document that says where he lives.
- **Escalation:** his rental contract: "Não chega"; his bills: "Não chega"; a selfie in front of his door: "Não chega".
- **Payoff (answer):** D stamps the whole tower at once, smiling: "Bem-vindo. [PT] (Welcome.)" Midnight fireworks: it took all year.
- **Button:** O kisses the stamp.
