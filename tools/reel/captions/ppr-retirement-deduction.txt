Saving into a PPR (Plano Poupança Reforma) in Portugal comes with an IRS deduction — and a real cost if you cash out too soon.

EBF art. 21.º sets a deduction of 20% of the amount you invest in the year, capped by your age on the date of each contribution:

· Under 35: capped at €400 (on up to €2.000 invested)
· 35 to 50: capped at €350 (on up to €1.750 invested)
· Over 50: capped at €300 (on up to €1.500 invested)

The contribution must fall within the calendar year — by 31 December — to count for that year's deduction. Contribution amounts are normally reported to Finanças by the PPR manager and pre-filled on your IRS return.

The condition: keep the money in for at least 5 years from each deposit, or redeem it only under the legally permitted conditions (retirement age, or specific hardship cases). Redeem outside those conditions, and EBF art. 21.º n.º 4 requires you to repay the deductions you claimed — increased by 10% for every full year or part-year since you claimed them.

What to do: if you're contributing for the tax benefit, check your age band's cap, contribute before 31 December, and know the 5-year rule before you touch the money early.

Hedges: this describes the tax rule only, not which PPR product to choose — that is not something we cover. We found no confirmed 2025/2026 change to these caps; treat any claim of a new cap as unverified until Finanças confirms it.

Source: EBF art. 21.º (Portal das Finanças).
