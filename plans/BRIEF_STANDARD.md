# Finkavo brief standard

The rolling-year plan is no longer generated by index arithmetic over a topic list. Every slot is an
authored brief that must earn its place. This document is the contract the validator enforces.

## The bar

> Hundreds of pages already explain what a NIF is. A Finkavo post exists only if a reader who already
> follows three other Portugal-admin accounts would still learn something they can act on.

A brief that merely defines a thing is rejected. A brief that states a rule everyone states is rejected.
A brief passes when it carries a **specific, checkable anchor** and answers a question a real person
asks at a real moment.

## Required structure

Every brief in `plans/brief-bank.json` has:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `id` | stable slug, unique across the bank |
| `pillar` | one of the 14 editorial pillars |
| `valueClass` | one of the seven classes below — determines what kind of value the post delivers |
| `title` | written, not templated; must not match any banned template pattern |
| `userQuestion` | the exact question a person would type or ask |
| `scenario` | the concrete situation the reader is in when they ask it |
| `audience` | who specifically, not "everyone living in Portugal" |
| `promise` | the single concrete thing the reader leaves with |
| `anchorFact` | the specific number, date, form, article, or portal path the post hinges on |
| `notObvious` | one line: why this is not what every other page already posts |
| `journeyStage` | which audience journey moment this serves |
| `source` | `canonicalUrl` + `authority` that must answer the brief exactly |
| `risk` | low / medium / high |
| `volatility` | `stable` \| `annual` \| `volatile` — drives reverification frequency |

## Value classes

Each class guarantees a different kind of added value. A pillar may not fill its quota from one class.

1. **`threshold`** — hinges on a specific figure, limit, rate, or bracket. The post is worthless without
   the number, which is exactly what makes it valuable.
2. **`scenario`** — one named, concrete situation walked end to end. Not "how renting works" but
   "your landlord will not give you a receipt and you need proof of address for AIMA".
3. **`costly_mistake`** — what goes wrong, what it actually costs, and how to undo it.
4. **`edge_case`** — the documented exception to the rule every other page states as absolute.
5. **`decision`** — X versus Y with the real criteria for choosing, not a definition of both.
6. **`deadline_action`** — date-locked; what to do inside a specific window.
7. **`insider`** — the non-obvious procedural fact: the step that is not on the form, the order
   operations must happen in, the thing that silently fails.

## Anchor facts and freshness

`anchorFact` is a **claim to be verified, never a fact to be trusted from the plan**. Portuguese
thresholds, rates and deadlines change annually and the plan is authored ahead of time. Each anchor
carries its own `verifyAt` URL, and generation must read the current value from that source. A brief
whose anchor cannot be confirmed against its source is held — it is never published with the planned
number.

`volatility` sets the reverification window:

- `stable` — procedural facts that rarely change (90 days)
- `annual` — figures set in the annual budget or updated each tax year (30 days)
- `volatile` — deadlines, campaign windows, portal availability (7 days)

## Banned patterns

The validator rejects any title matching these, because they produced 74% of the old plan:

- `: a plain-English explanation`
- `: a practical step-by-step checklist`
- `: mistakes, exceptions and edge cases`
- `: one golden tip and what to verify`
- `{topic} for {audience}` where `{audience}` is the pillar's default audience string
- Any title beginning `What a … is` or `… basics`

## Voice, by risk

The hook energy that earns a save on an evergreen consumer post reads as clickbait on a tax or
immigration post, where the reader is deciding whether to trust us with a real decision.

- **low / medium risk** — punchy and direct is encouraged. Contrarian framing, direct address and
  a sharp turn are all fair: *"The capped-fee bank account branches will not offer you"*.
- **high risk** (tax, immigration, legal, deadlines, eligibility, health) — plain, declarative and
  authoritative. The title states what the reader will learn. No second-person hook, no two-sentence
  reveal, no exclamation. Specificity carries the interest instead:
  *"When IVA starts applying after you cross the Artigo 53 ceiling"*.

Hype vocabulary (`secret`, `hack`, `trick`, `nobody tells you`, `guaranteed`, `shocking`) is banned at
every risk level. Being genuinely non-obvious is the differentiator; claiming to be is not.

## Unverifiable briefs

If no official source can be found that answers a brief exactly, the brief is **dropped and replaced**,
not retained in a held state. The bank contains only briefs whose anchor has a live, verified official
page behind it. `scripts/validate-brief-bank.mjs --check-sources` fetches every `canonicalUrl` and
`verifyAt`; a 404 or 410 fails the build. Some official sites (notably `bportugal.pt`) reject
non-browser agents with 403 — that is reported as "verify in a browser", never treated as dead.

## Uniqueness

Identity is `pillar | userQuestion | audience | valueClass`. No two briefs may share it. Unlike the old
plan, the same underlying subject may appear more than once **only** when the scenario and value class
genuinely differ — "IMI is split into instalments above a threshold" (`threshold`) and "you moved house
mid-year and the IMI bill went to the old address" (`scenario`) are different posts. Six copies of one
checklist are not.

## Evidence terms

`evidenceTerms` are derived from the brief's `anchorFact` and `source`, never by chopping words out of
the title. The old glossary matched `/rent/` unanchored and tagged parental-leave posts with tenancy
law; term derivation must use word-boundary matching and must fail loudly rather than guess.
